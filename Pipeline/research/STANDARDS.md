# The standards behind EPIC.md (31 Oct 2026, night)

His words: "Apply correct standard proven systems engineering principles in this in the requirements and find those standards online for game dev and smoothness and combat and movement and animation and art style rtc". In his video: "It's just deciding what working means and proving it at every step."

This file says what each standard asks and what we do about it. `EPIC.md` turns that into requirements. A label like `[Fiedler04]` is how a row in EPIC cites a source here. `QS-A5` means checkpoint A5 in `QUALITY_SOURCES.md` (34 checkpoints, read at source on 28 Oct, not repeated here).

**How far each source was read:**

| Mark | Means |
|---|---|
| **S** | Read at the source: the page, or the paper's full text |
| **P** | Only the publisher's public page. The full text is paid, or the talk is a video |
| **2** | Only someone else's summary. A lead, not a fact |
| **X** | Couldn't be read, or wasn't. Named so nobody thinks it was used |

A number nobody published is marked (ours) in EPIC. This file is for study and never ships. It names games and studios only as citations; none of those names go in the game, its listing or a reel.

**Clean room.** On 31 Oct a research pass read a decompilation and a disassembly of two console games. `steal_skill.md` §0 bars those "in any form, for any purpose". The copies are deleted, research struck every row (23:41), and `AGENTS.md` carries the rule. Nothing in this file or in EPIC.md comes from them. The full record is `CLEANROOM.md`. EPIC Q16 and `web_check/static.mjs` hold the line from now on.

## The short version: what each builder takes from this

| Owner | Lessons | From |
|---|---|---|
| **movement** | Scale the stride with speed; don't just raise the clip's rate (the audit's core defect). Up to sprint, cap cadence at 5 steps/s: the fastest measured human rate is 4.32/s, at 12.34 m/s. Real sprinters get faster through longer flight, not faster legs; past 13 m/s he's superhuman, and M6 sets its own rate. Give the hips a rise and fall, or he floats. Player wind-up is short, at most 0.1 s to leaving the ground; `takeoff` is now 0.07 s, so keep it there. Coyote time has a published number, 0.1 s, in the file Celeste's makers released under MIT; the jump buffer's length isn't published, so ours is marked. Inertialization can give knee pops, so read the knees at switches. Bounds in cm per frame mean nothing unless the frame is fixed: use 1/60 s. | `[EpicWarp]` `[Mackala13]` `[Weyand00]` `[Williams01]` `[Lasseter87]` `[Thorson]` `[CelesteSrc]` `[Holden23]` |
| **combat** | Write frame data as startup, active, recovery, counted the way fighting games count, with startup including the first hitting frame. Enemies get long wind-ups, the player short ones. His reference never freezes the whole frame: bodies hold 0.03 s on a light blow and up to 0.10 s on a heavy one, and inputs pressed during it stay buffered. The next action starts 0.13-0.33 s after contact. Tokens limit how many attack at once, and token counts are a difficulty setting. Never start an attack while the player can't respond. Hit boxes must match what's seen. Sound never carries a telegraph alone: a muted phone is the normal case. Flashes count against the three-a-second limit, so P11's contact flash stays small: at its biggest the reference's bright area is 7.4% of the frame. | `[FrameData]` `[RefClip]` `[Lasseter87]` `[Thompson18]` `[ISTQB]` `QS-E3` |
| **missions** | Teach each power in four beats: try it safely, use it harder, a twist, then a test. Every source of pearls and relics needs a sink. Objectives must be reviewable at any time, with a log of what's done and a count of what's left. Change difficulty any time without losing progress. Cinematics must pause and skip. Remove repeated information from dialogue. | `[Hayashida12]` `[Cook21]` `[XAG109]` `[XAG108]` `[Sasko23]` |
| **look** | Value before colour: the Warden needs 3:1 against what's behind him at 60 px tall. Shadows go cool, never black. Compile programs before play (done) and avoid `getError` and `readPixels` on the ship path. 4× MSAA is fine on tile GPUs. Triangles are the budget most at risk: 389k at 1.3 MP is about 3 pixels a triangle, while Arm asks for 10-20. | `[TF2-07]` `QS-E2` `[MDN-WebGL]` `[Arm-GPU]` |
| **world** | Every biome needs one thing seen from far off, and the land should hide and reveal what's next. Mesh levels of detail are what keep the triangle count honest as the map grows. The CDN compresses text only: `.glb`, `.bin` and `.png` go raw, so every MB of binary costs nearly a second at 10 Mbps. Load what the first view doesn't need after first play. | `[BOTW17]` `[Arm-GPU]` `EPIC §15` |
| **qa** | A check's sample must be big enough for its statistic: a p99 needs thousands of frames. Report frame time in ms (median, p95, p99, worst, with the count), never fps alone. A median on a 60 Hz display reads 60 even when four frames in ten miss. Test frame pacing with stamp lists, including 30 Hz, which is what an iPhone in Low Power Mode gives a page. A check that crashed must say it didn't run: "0 FAIL, 0 ok" reads like a result. | `[Percentiles]` `[WebSmooth]` `[WebKitLPM]` `[NASA.ver]` |
| **lead** | Don't call a thing done without its objective evidence. A number that isn't known yet gets a best estimate marked TBR, with who closes it and by when. After a baseline, a change goes through one person, with its impact written. Reviews are short gates, not meetings. Priorities only mean something if some are not Must. | `[SEBoK]` `[NASA.req]` `[NASA.cm]` `[NASA.rev]` `[MoSCoW]` |

## What was looked for and not found

- A published length for the jump buffer or a parry window for touch. Those numbers in EPIC are (ours). Hit-stop comes from `[RefClip]`, coyote time from `[CelesteSrc]`.
- A published standard for foot slide in cm, joint speed in °/s, or anticipation length. `steal_skill.md` §2's bounds are the method's, from a skate game, and are used as such.
- A published limit for iPhone Safari memory (QS Part 5 says the same).
- A published smoothness pass line for the web. `[WebSmooth]` publishes the metric but no threshold.
- Exact wording of ISO 15288, 29148, 25010 and 29119, and the INCOSE guide and handbook: all paid. Nothing here claims conformance to them.
- Any source for shape language worth citing.
