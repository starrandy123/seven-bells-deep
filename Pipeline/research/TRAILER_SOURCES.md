# How shipped gameplay trailers show a fight (2 Nov 2026)

His words: "The clips are bad. The bosses are not fighting back ... We need actual fighting ... I need the video in landscape" · "Did you look at example game trailers like this. We need actual action and ragdoll". Read 2 Nov by a research pass. Game sources are numbered (T1-T6); their titles and links are in the owner's memory record `reference_trailer_sources_2026_11_02.md`, as his rule keeps titles out of repo files. Measured from the trailers' own storyboard frames against a 10% grid; frames were 0.6-1 s apart on short trailers and ~5 s on long demos, so hit counts are estimates.

| Rule | Source |
|---|---|
| Demos never cut inside a fight: one take of 50 s or more; one full exchange (telegraph, dodge or counter, the enemy launched and left lying) every 3-5 s | T1, T2 gameplay demos |
| A fight ends on a 3-4 s low push-in two-shot, both figures 70-80% of the frame, sky behind | T2 |
| A boss's attack and the hero's reaction share the frame: the boss 50-90% of it, the hero 5-10%, its fist toward the lens and the hero thrown; bolts crossing the frame at the hero | T2, T1 |
| The hero's share of frame height: small fights 35-45% (lens just above head height, ~10° down, enemies 20-30%); crowds 15-25%; bosses 5-25%; vistas 60-70% on the left third | measured across T1-T5 |
| Hit-stop scales with the blow (a fighting game's damage x 0.65 + 6 frames, at most 30); shake the victim, keep the attacker moving, freeze only for big moments | SmashWiki, "Hitlag"; a game director's published videos on hit-stop |
| Set the camera's distance and FOV together from its pitch, wider for low angles | GDC Vault 1020460 (a camera talk) |
| Flight is filmed in chase-camera takes of 20-25 s with the hero 8-12% tall, centred just below the middle; a river chase rides ~1 m over the water with the horizon 38% from the top | T5, T2 |
| Short trailers: the hero 1-4 s in; montage shots 0.8-1.3 s; black beats 0.5-2 s; the title at 78-87% of runtime, then a 2-6 s closing beat | measured across five short trailers |
| Show the hook in the first 6 s; tell a short story inside each long take; show a thing three times; pace in waves ("If a trailer feels high energy all the time, then none of it will feel high energy"); "Don't cut fast for the sake of cutting fast" | Derek Lieu, derek-lieu.com/blog |

His own word on the cut sets the take at about 3 s ("This is why I said 3 seconds"), so each 3 s cut holds one full exchange.
