## Birds: read at source

- Reynolds 1987 (boids: avoidance, velocity matching, centering, in priority) and 1999 (steering behaviours: offset pursuit, arrival).
- Ballerini et al. 2008: each starling watches its nearest 6.5 ± 0.9 neighbours, whatever the distance.
- Hildenbrandt et al. 2010, StarDisplay: a starling murmuration's numbers.
- Pennycuick 1996: flap rate f = m^(3/8) g^(1/2) b^(-23/24) S^(-1/3) rho^(-3/8). A bird drawn k times life size flaps at f / sqrt(k).
- Sato et al. 2009, albatross flap schedules; Weimerskirch et al. 2016, frigatebirds that hardly beat; Sachs 2012, dynamic soaring.
- Portugal et al. 2014: a V's slot is 0.8 span back and 0.89 span out, and each bird beats so its wingtip follows the same air.
- Cheney et al. 2020: wings as a suspension, the head steady in the air.
- The three.js birds example (MIT) read for method only.

## Built: `web/js/birds.js`

Wings with a shoulder, a wrist and a hand bent in the vertex shader: on the upstroke the wrist folds and the hand sweeps back. Bank from the turn, atan(v²/(r g)). Frigatebirds ring the volcano's plume, where the air rises, and lift him inside the ring. Cranes cross between the isles in a ragged V with one arm longer; he can slot in and they set the pace. Tropicbirds, a real volcano bird, over the crater lake.
